# Learning / Codex-Spark — LinkedIn Posts

---

I ran an analysis on 809 of my AI conversations. One finding stood out: as volume went up, quality went down. More questions, less precision. More output, less understanding.

I called it "more volume, less care." It is the central trap in AI-assisted learning.

Codex-Spark runs at 1000+ tokens per second on Cerebras WSE-3. That is 15x faster than standard Codex. At that speed, the bottleneck is no longer the answer — it is your ability to form the next question.

This changes what it means to learn with AI. The question is no longer "how do I get information fast?" It becomes "how do I form better questions?" The constraint shifts from the model to you.

That is the learning treadmill. It matches your pace. You set the speed. The machine keeps up. Stop thinking and it stops with you.

The question is whether you have the discipline to run it slowly enough to actually learn — and not just accumulate answers.

---

The best tutors respond fast. Not just intellectually — physically. They read confusion in real time and redirect before the student loses the thread.

That responsiveness has always been expensive. Private tutors, office hours, small classes. These are access advantages that compound over time. Not because wealthier students are smarter — because their questions get answered faster and more precisely.

A student in a resource-limited school now has the same on-demand tutoring tool as one with a private tutor at $200/hour. For "I do not understand this at 11pm before an exam" — they now get the same response time.

Codex-Spark can generate five different explanations of any concept in under ten seconds. Mathematical. Intuitive. Historical. Practical. Visual. The student picks what clicks.

A human tutor gives you one explanation and feels slightly frustrated if you still do not get it. Spark gives you five without social cost. No embarrassment. No impatience. Unlimited attempts.

The bottleneck in learning used to be access. It is shifting toward will.

That is not a small change. It is worth building real educational tools around.

---

I shipped faster last year than any year prior. More features, more conversations, more output.

My spec quality went from 0.49 to 0.21 as volume increased. I was moving faster and thinking less. The speed was hiding the shallowness.

The same dynamic applies to learning with fast AI models. Reading a clear explanation feels like understanding it. It is not. The gap between "that made sense" and "I can explain it tomorrow" is enormous.

When I use Spark for learning, I now impose artificial friction. After getting an explanation, I close the chat and write what I understood in my own words. Not the model's words. Mine. Within three sentences I usually discover I understood less than I thought.

Then I go back with a more precise question.

The struggle is not a bug in learning. It is the mechanism. Instant answers eliminate the productive discomfort of not knowing — which is exactly where understanding forms.

Use fast models to accelerate the exposure phase. But do not skip the working-with-it phase. That part cannot be sped up.

The speed feels like productivity. Know the difference.

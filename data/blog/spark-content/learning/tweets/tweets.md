# Learning / Codex-Spark — Tweets

1. When answers are instant, the bottleneck becomes your ability to form the next question. Spark changed what I have to be good at.

2. The speed feels like productivity. It is not. I covered twenty concepts in an hour and could explain none of them afterward.

3. 1000 tokens per second means the model responds before the question finishes forming in your head. The learning loop becomes continuous, not episodic.

4. Every student now has a tutor that responds at the speed of thought. The bottleneck shifted from access to will.

5. Five explanations of the same concept in nine seconds. You pick the one that clicks. That is what personalized learning actually looks like.

6. Flow state while learning used to require an expert who could answer every question in real time. That was expensive and rare. Now it is not.

7. "Let me Google that" breaks flow. Three tabs later you have forgotten what you were learning. Spark eliminates that entire pattern.

8. The embarrassment barrier in learning is real. Students stop asking because they are afraid of seeming slow. Spark has no frustration on the other side.

9. More volume, less care. My data showed it. 809 conversations analyzed. Fast answers create the illusion of learning. Actual learning still requires friction.

10. Old learning loop: ask, wait, forget context, get answer, reconstruct. New loop: ask, answer, deeper ask, answer. The loop accelerates until you run out of questions.

11. Close Spark. Write what you understood. If you cannot explain it without the chat, you did not learn it.

12. One deep thread beats ten shallow ones. I learned this the hard way after weeks of coverage without comprehension.

13. The struggle is not a bug in learning. It is the mechanism. Instant answers eliminate the productive discomfort of not knowing.
